Excel is used as a database constantly, usually without anyone deciding to. A sheet gets a header row, people add records to it, and a database is what it has become.

For a single person or a small team that can be perfectly reasonable. It is visible, everyone can already use it, and it costs nothing. Problems arrive with concurrent editing, with data that needs validating, and with history that needs keeping.

The honest answer is that there is a threshold, and it is worth knowing which side of it you are on. Part of what we do is say when a spreadsheet has outgrown itself - and when it hasn't, which is more often than you might expect.
